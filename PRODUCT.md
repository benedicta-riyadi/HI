# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Students who want to build something real but can't do it alone — the one with
an idea and no team, and the one with skills (design, code, writing, research,
ops) looking for a project worth their time. They're early-career and
ambition-rich but time-poor and a little scared to start: a half-formed idea, a
free weekend, a portfolio that needs proof. They arrive from a classmate's
share, a campus group, or a "where do I find people to build with?" search, and
decide in a couple of minutes whether posting an idea — or joining one — feels
worth the leap.

## Product Purpose

pioHub connects students with complementary skills and matching commitment so
every idea gets the people it needs and every builder gets a place to start.
Post the project you keep thinking about and say who you need; join an existing
one with what you already have. Owners approve who joins, so teams are made of
people who actually want to be there, and every finished project lives on your
profile as proof you don't just plan — you try. Success looks like a student
going from "I have an idea" to "we're building it" in their first sitting, and
coming back with something to show for it.

## Brand Personality

Energetic and Gen-Z playful — the friend who texts "wait this is actually a
great idea, let's just start" at 1am and means it. Confident, warm, and a little
bold, but never hype-y or corporate. Voice is direct and encouraging, in
sentence case, speaking to ambition without pressure: starting is framed as
low-stakes and joining as just as valuable as founding. Three words: bold,
encouraging, in-motion.

## Anti-references

- **The generic purple/violet AI-gradient hero with a glassmorphic card** — the
  default "tech startup" reflex the user explicitly called out. No lavender
  gradients, no frosted-glass cards as decoration.
- Corporate enterprise SaaS: stiff navy-and-gray, feature-grid landing pages
  that feel like they're selling to a procurement team, not a student.
- Identical icon-heading-text card grids repeated down the page (the current
  landing leans on this — worth breaking up).
- Childish / over-cute: mascots, toy-bubble shapes, anything that reads as too
  juvenile to trust with a real project.

## Design Principles

- **Lower the barrier to starting.** "You don't need a perfect plan." Every
  surface should make posting or joining feel like a small, safe first step, not
  a commitment to ship a company.
- **Momentum over polish.** Reward action, joining, and finishing — celebrate
  the project that exists over the plan that's perfect.
- **Joining is as valued as founding.** Design the contributor's path with the
  same weight as the owner's; complementary skills are the whole point.
- **Show real people and real projects, not abstractions.** Concrete ideas,
  names, and roles beat stock illustration and adjectives.
- **Bold but credible.** Personality lives in color, type, and copy — energetic
  enough to feel made-for-students, disciplined enough to trust.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥ 4.5:1, large text ≥ 3:1, visible focus states on
every interactive element, full keyboard operability. Honor
`prefers-reduced-motion` with non-animated alternatives. Don't encode meaning in
color alone — pair role/status color with a text label or icon (the role-tag
system already does this).
